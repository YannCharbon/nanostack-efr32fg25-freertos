# Weekend LFN campaign on the FG25 kits (2026-11-07/08)

This campaign runs, in one session on the four FG25 kits, every LFN check that still needs hardware. The phases are ordered so that each hour of kit time serves several checks. The runner is `tests/hw/run_weekend.sh`. It takes the kits' lock, flashes the images, runs the phases in turn and writes a summary with one line per check.

The campaign has three aims. The first is to close the LFN work (FAN 1.1 LFN in our stack, its firmware and the black-box comparison with Silicon Labs' stack), which remains open for one reason that a desk can address: Silicon Labs' LFN has never run on our kits with its real Balanced and Eco timers. The second is to check on hardware two stack changes of 2026-11-05: the removal of the idle housekeeping wakes (only a 12-minute kit check so far), and the random jitter on the LFN's discovery sweep (LFNs that power up together). The third is to confirm, over long silences, a stack fix under preparation, without which a downlink to an Eco LFN after 10 h or more of silence is lost in the emulator. The weak-link test near sensitivity is not part of it: it needs the MTS bench.

Total duration: **about 26 h** (for example Saturday 08:00 to Sunday 10:00). The duration needs the owner's approval (section 7).

All our images must come from one stack commit that contains both GS11 and the fix of the Eco long-silence downlink. `build_weekend.sh` records the stack and firmware commits and an MD5 of every image in `build/wk-images.txt`, and the runner prints that file before it starts.

**Profiles.** The new firmware option `FW_LFN_PROFILE` (`test`, `balanced` or `eco`) applies Silicon Labs' profile values, as the emulator's `sim_utils/vtime/lfn_profile.sh` sets them, to the parent (LFN broadcast interval and sync period, registration lifetime) and to the LFN (listen interval, parent maintenance, key and DHCPv6 timers, sync-slot-only listening). Balanced: listen 60 s, LFN broadcast slot every 60 s, a sync frame every 15 min. Eco: listen 300 s, LFN broadcast slot every 600 s, a sync frame every 10 h, with listening only in sync slots. Without the option, the application objects are identical to those of firmware `40919d5`. The images differ only by the CLI's new optional argument to `ping` (`ping <address> [count] [size] [interval ms] [wait s]`), which waits for replies one listen interval long.

## 2. Phases

| Phase | Kits | Duration | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | All four, our network: border router, router, two LFNs under the router | About 1 h 05 | Flashing with mass erase; 10 paired LFN resets; AEM dumps of both LFNs at rest; 3 router restarts with traffic |
| B | Two pairs at the same time | About 16 h 30 | Pair X: our LFN with the Balanced settings (about 3 h), then with the Eco settings (about 13 h 30). Pair Y: Silicon Labs' LFN, Balanced and Eco under our border router (about 4 h 45), then Eco under `wsbrd` (about 11 h 30) |
| C | All four, our network as in phase A | About 8 h | Long idle: downlinks to both LFNs after 8 silences of 10 min to 2 h; AEM windows on both LFN kits; AEM dumps on 440290182 |

The phases run in the order A, B, C. Phase B starts in the daytime, so that any failure of the Silicon Labs procedures, which have not run before in this form, shows while the kits are attended. Phase C reproduces the overnight run of 2026-11-04 with the new stack, so its figures compare directly.

### Phase A: our network (about 1 h 05)

- **A1, LFNs that power up together** (`run_hw_gs11.sh`, about 20 min). The border router and the router start; once the router holds its LGTK, both LFNs are reset at the same instant (two `commander` resets started together). This is repeated 10 times. The first pair joins cold through EAP-TLS (the kits were erased); the next ones rejoin with their keys in flash. After each pair, the border router sends a UDP datagram to each LFN, which answers it. Recorded: each LFN's join time (from its reset to the end of its join) and the offset between the two boots.
- **A2, the LFNs at rest** (about 6 min). One AEM dump of 240 s on each LFN kit at the same time, with no console open and no traffic: windows, timer wakes per minute, the short 1.02 s wakes and the floor.
- **A3, router restarts with keys in flash** (`run_hw_lfn_dl.sh`, 3 runs, about 12 min each). Ten datagrams 30 s apart to each LFN; the router is reset before the 4th. Recorded: the router's LGTK after its reset, when traffic resumes and how many datagrams were lost.

### Phase B: two pairs at the same time (about 16 h 30)

The two pairs use different PHYs and network names (EU mode 3 for pair X, PHY mode 1 with plan 32 for pair Y), so they share the band without joining each other. A frame of one pair can be lost to a frame of the other; the downlink checks wait one listen interval and more, so a single loss does not fail a check.

**Pair X: our LFN with Silicon Labs' profiles** (border router 440235475, LFN 440290182, `run_hw_lfn_dl.sh`):
- **BX1, Balanced** (about 3 h): cold join (up to 45 min allowed; the DHCPv6 delay alone can reach 300 s); 5 downlinks 30 min apart, each answered by the LFN; an AEM window of 60 s every 10 min; a 300 s AEM dump every hour (GS10 method: windows, timer wakes, floor).
- **BX2, Eco** (about 13 h 30): cold join (up to 90 min allowed); a downlink right after the join, one an hour later, one after **11 h of silence**, which spans the first 10 h sync, and one 30 min later; an AEM window of 60 s every 15 min; a 310 s AEM dump every 3 h. The downlink after 11 h is the hardware check of the stack fix of 2026-11-05. Over these hours the LFN has not yet measured its clock rate (two sync frames 10 h apart are needed), so its windows widen at 50 ppm: the current is that of an Eco LFN's first hours, not its steady state.

**Pair Y: Silicon Labs' LFN with its real profiles** (parent 440240046, LFN 440235509, `lfn_blackbox/s8_run.py`). Before each run, the LFN is configured over its console (network "hw5lfn", +10 dBm, the profile, saved) and power-cycled, because a profile change needs a reboot (black-box phase 1, item 39). Its credentials are then cleared and it joins.
- **BY1, Balanced under our border router** (about 2 h 15): join (up to 45 min); 30 min without traffic (the parent's clock rate needs two syncs 15 min apart); then for 90 min, every 15 min, a downlink (the border router's echo request) and an uplink (the LFN's CLI `wisun ping`), each awaited one listen interval plus 20 s; an AEM window every 10 min; two 300 s dumps.
- **BY2, Eco under our border router** (about 2 h 30): join (up to 90 min); for 2 h, every 30 min, a downlink and an uplink (each awaited 320 s); an AEM window every 15 min; two 660 s dumps.
- **BY3, Eco under `wsbrd`** (about 11 h 30): `wsbrd` with Eco's parent settings (`s8_eco.conf`: LFN broadcast slot every 600 s, sync every 60 slots); a downlink and an uplink after the join, then the next ones after 10.2 h of silence, across the first sync; an AEM window every 15 min; three 660 s dumps. This gives Silicon Labs' own behaviour in the case of the Eco stack fix.

Silicon Labs' LFN under `wsbrd` with the Balanced profile is not repeated: black-box phase 1 measured its "automatic" profile, which behaves as Balanced on the LFN side (28.8 µA, phase 1 items 37–38).

### Phase C: long idle with downlinks (about 8 h)

The network of phase A again (all kits erased and flashed). Downlinks to both LFNs after silences of 600, 1 800, 3 600, 4 000, 5 400, 7 200, 3 600 and 1 800 s (7.8 h, 16 downlinks in all). AEM windows of 60 s every 30 min on both LFN kits at the same time; a 240 s dump at 100 kHz on 440290182 every 3 h. This is the setup of the overnight run of 2026-11-04 (border router, router, two LFNs under it, default LFN settings), so the deliveries, latencies and currents compare directly. The dumps over the night show whether the timer-wake rate stays at 1.0 a minute, and how the floor moves over hours.

**What the kits cannot show.** Two commander resets leave the two boots tens to hundreds of milliseconds apart, while two LFNs collide only if their discovery sweeps start within one frame's airtime (about 5 ms). The kits therefore show that both LFNs still join promptly with the jitter, which is a non-regression check. The colliding case itself stays the emulator's (its LFN tests with `SPREAD=0`). The summary gives the measured boot offsets.

## 4. What the AEM records, and when

| When | Kit | What | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A2 | 440290182, 440235509 | One 240 s dump each (100 kHz, 10 kHz) | Timer wakes after the housekeeping change; 1.02 s wakes; floor of the day |
| BX1 | 440290182 | 60 s window every 10 min; 300 s dump every hour | Balanced current; window decomposition |
| BX2 | 440290182 | 60 s window every 15 min; 310 s dump every 3 h | Eco current in its first hours; window widening at 50 ppm |
| BY1–BY3 | 440235509 | 60 s window every 10–15 min; 300 s (Balanced) or 660 s (Eco) dumps | Silicon Labs' LFN current and window periods per profile |
| C1 | Both LFN kits | 60 s windows every 30 min; on 440290182 a 240 s dump every 3 h | Current over the night, timer-wake rate over hours, floor variation |

Each dump is analysed as soon as it ends (`aem_floor.py`, then `aem_wakes.py`): mean current, floor, windows per minute and their recurring periods, timer wakes per minute and their charge, sync-frame processing wakes, short wakes and the share of them on a 1.02 s grid. The compressed CSV is kept. The AEM reads the board controller's current monitor and makes no debugger access to the target, so it does not disturb the LFN. No RAM is read during the campaign: polling an LFN's RAM made it miss sync frames on 2026-11-04.

Two open questions may be answered at no extra cost:
- **The 1.02 s wakes.** The dumps of Silicon Labs' LFN from black-box phase 1 (kit 440235509, 2026-10-28) show the same short wakes on a 1.02 s grid (58 a minute), so they are not specific to our stack. Pair Y adds dumps of Silicon Labs' LFN under our border router and under `wsbrd`, for comparison with our LFN's.
- **The floor's day-to-day variation.** Phase C gives the floor of 440290182 every 3 h through the night, and A2 and BX1 give it on the Saturday. Temperature is not recorded, because the PC has no sensor at the kits.

## 5. Runner and outputs

```bash
cd nanostack-efr32fg25-freertos
tests/hw/build_weekend.sh                     # once the stack commit is known; build only
tests/hw/run_weekend.sh --dry-run             # every command, no hardware, no lock, a few seconds
tests/hw/run_weekend.sh --detach              # the campaign, in its own session
tests/hw/run_weekend.sh --summary <out dir>   # summary of an output, at any time
```

- **Output:** `.deps/captures/weekend-2026-11-07/` in the root repository (on disk, not in `/tmp`), one directory per step (`A1_gs11`, `A2_aem`, `A3_rnreset_1..3`, `BX1_balanced`, `BX2_eco`, `BY1_p3_balanced`, `BY2_p3_eco`, `BY3_p1_eco`, `C1_idle`), `run.log`, `flash.log` and `summary.txt`. The summary is rebuilt after every step, so partial results are readable during the run.
- **Summary lines:** `PASS:`, `FAIL:`, `FIGURE:` (measured values with their reference) and `NOTE:` (a step not run or not finished, consoles lost and restarted), then a total.
- **Lock:** the runner holds the kits' lock (`flock` on `locks/silabs-kits`) for the whole run, and writes the start, the end and the images left on each kit in `silabs-kits.notes`.
- **Robustness:**
  - `--detach` starts the runner with `setsid` and `nohup`, so closing the terminal does not stop it.
  - Each console runs under a watchdog that starts it again if it is lost, appending to its log. On 2026-11-04 a lost border-router console stopped a long run, and the USB link of 440290182 dropped once.
  - Before it starts, the runner checks that no other process holds a kit's console port.
  - A command to a console that is not reading gives up after 5 s instead of blocking.
  - A step that fails does not stop the next ones.
- **Stopping:** `kill <pid>` (in `locks/weekend.pid`) releases the lock and writes the summary. The kits keep the images of the current phase.

**Tests of the runner (2026-11-05).**
- **Dry run:** the full campaign, about 1 230 printed actions in 7 s.
- **Summary on recorded outputs:** the overnight run of 2026-11-04 as phase C gives 16 of 16 and 15 of 16, latencies 0.7–5.5 s and 0.3–6.0 s, AEM 24.08–25.23 µA, the miss after 4 000 s and the console lost on 440290182, as documented.
- **Wake classifier on the AEM records of 2026-11-05:** 4.0 timer wakes a minute at 6.09 µC before the housekeeping change, and 1.0 at 6.04 µC after (documented: 4.0 and 1.0, 6.06 and 6.05 µC).
- **Window periods:** [6, 10] s and [5, 10] s for our LFN; [10, 30] s for Silicon Labs' shortened Eco of phase 1 (documented: 10 s broadcast slots, 30.002 s unicast).
- **Silicon Labs' console patterns, on the phase 3 logs:** joins of 48–113 s and 8 of 8 echo replies each way, as documented.
- **Console watchdog, on a pseudo-terminal:** console killed, then restarted with its log continued.
- **Not tested on the kits:** the new firmware option and ping argument (built only), and the Silicon Labs set-up sequence as a whole (each command was used in phase 1).

## 7. Owner decisions and set-up

1. **Approval of the duration: about 26 h of continuous kit use** (for example Saturday 08:00 to Sunday 10:00).
   - **What it does:** the runner flashes all four kits several times, resets them, and runs the phases above unattended. Nobody else can use the kits during that time.
   - **Why now:** it closes the LFN work (Silicon Labs' real profiles), checks the two stack changes of 2026-11-05 on hardware, and confirms the Eco long-silence fix.
   - **Yes:** results on Sunday morning in `summary.txt`.
   - **No:** a shorter campaign without the Eco long silences (phase A, BX1, BY1, BY2 and a 4 h phase C: about 13 h) loses the hardware check of the Eco fix and Silicon Labs' behaviour across a 10 h sync.
   - **Recommendation:** yes. Phases B and C cannot be shortened without dropping a check, because the Eco checks need a silence longer than the 10 h sync period.
2. **Option, not recommended: our Eco LFN in its steady state.**
   - **What it does:** an Eco LFN measures its clock rate only after two sync frames 10 h apart. The current after that (emulator 12.10–12.74 µA) needs about 12 h more of BX2 (`ECO_GAPS="3600 39600 1800 43200 600"`), which would end the campaign around Sunday 22:00.
   - **Recommendation:** no. The emulator covers this case with 72–96 h runs, and the power formula is within 0–3 % of the kits with the default settings.
3. **Set-up:** nothing to change on the bench.
   - The four kits stay connected and side by side as they are, with the AEM switches as now. The AEMs of 440290182 and 440235509 measure.
   - The PC must not suspend during the run.
   - No other tool (Simplicity Studio, a terminal on a kit's port) may be open on the kits.
